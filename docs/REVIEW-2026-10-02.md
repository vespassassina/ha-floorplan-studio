# Review, 2026-10-02: product, design and the 3D question

Scope: ha-floorplan-studio 0.12.15 (0.12.16 built, not released). Read: the
README, SPEC, schema, CHANGELOG, the 2026-09-24 and 09-25 reviews, the
current `npm run shots` output, and `src/core/schema.ts`. Forum and GitHub
evidence from one research pass today.

**What this review could not do.** Reddit refused scripted access: no Reddit
evidence. The web search tool was broken, so the forum evidence comes from
the HA community JSON and the GitHub API only. No vote counts exist on the
threads found, so ranking is by views, likes, stars and issue comments, which
is judgement. I could not open the editor in the browser pane (navigation
was denied), so UI findings come from the shots and the code, not from
driving it. Nothing here was run on a real Home Assistant.

## Verdict

The 2D product is strong and ahead of the field on the thing users complain
about most: setup effort. The gaps are no longer features. They are
**discovery**, **polish on small rooms**, and **one request the spec
excludes: 3D**. A 3D view is possible, and a procedural one fits this product
better than it fits anyone else's. It should be a second view of the same
layout, not a second product.

## 2. Findings

### Glitches and bugs seen or inferred

1. **README pictures are stale.** `docs/img/card-overview.png` still shows
   the pre-0.12.15 view: the garden clipped at the right edge. It is the
   first thing a new visitor sees. Retake the images with `npm run shots`.
2. **The Active list covers the plan.** In the card shots the list sits over
   the Living room. A wall tablet user can fold it, but a first look on a
   phone loses a quarter of the plan to it. Default it folded below a width
   threshold, or dock it outside the plan.
3. **Small rooms still collide.** "Garden" and "Garden pond" overprint in the
   demo. 0.12.16 keeps names inside rooms and smaller, which lowers the odds
   but does not solve a name wider than its room. Needed: shrink the text to
   fit, or move it to a leader line outside.
4. **Label opacity changes contrast.** Half-transparent names on a busy
   texture or on the lit pool may drop below readable. Check on the `light`
   and `beach-house` themes, and on a dim tablet, before keeping 50%.
5. **Release script hazard (mine).** On 2026-10-02 a chained release command
   tagged and pushed `v0.12.16` on the old main after the merge failed. The
   Release workflow failed and nothing shipped, but the bad tag is still on
   GitHub. Fix: release steps run one at a time, each checked, tag last.
   Add this to `docs/WORKFLOW.md`.
6. **Opus review skipped twice** (0.12.15, 0.12.16). The workflow requires
   it. Not a bug in the product; a hole in the process.
7. **Unverified from the 09-25 review:** whether a card with several hundred
   devices stays smooth on a phone. No performance number exists anywhere in
   the repo.

### Design and appeal

- **Strong:** thirteen themes, a theme that follows HA, role-generated
  palettes, a calm blueprint default, a state language that reads at a
  glance (aura, red-dashed open door, fading motion).
- **Weak:** the plan reads as a *drawing*, not a *home*. Next to the 3D
  dashboards that draw the likes, it looks technical. Room colours and
  textures help; depth does not exist. This is the real source of the 3D
  requests: people want a dashboard they show off.
- **Weak:** the editor is dense: Filter, Add, Draw, View, Edit, File, Help,
  then a side panel. Fine for a maker, heavy for a first-time user. The Help
  button and the "Load demo" first click are the right idea; a first-run
  tour of three steps would pay off.
- **Weak:** discoverability. Five stars. A custom repository only, not in
  the HACS default list. The 09-24 review said this was the top issue; it
  still is.

## 3. The 3D question

**Is it possible? Yes, with a limit.**

What users mean by 3D, from the threads:
1. Pre-rendered images from Blender or Sweet Home 3D, swapped by state.
   The most common DIY route, and the most work to build and to change.
2. A real-time scene from a user-supplied model (floor3d-card: OBJ or GLB;
   3Dash: GLB; DT3D). floor3d is the best known (612 stars, 641 posts, 303
   likes) and **unmaintained since 2024-04**, with open bugs for rendering
   failures, door state, RGB lamps, performance and multi-floor selection.
3. A Sweet Home 3D wireframe render that loads SH3D files.

The pain in all of them is the same: **making and re-making the model**, and
re-binding entities to mesh names after each export. Our layout already has
what a simple 3D view needs: room polygons, wall edges with kinds, doors and
openings with positions, stairs, furniture with size and rotation, devices at
x,y, floors in order.

What it does not have: **wall height, floor elevation, door and window
heights, sill heights, ceiling height.** The schema has none (checked in
`src/core/schema.ts`). A first version would use fixed defaults (say 2.5 m
walls, 2.1 m doors, 0.9 m sills, 2.7 m per floor) and expose one height per
floor later.

**Recommended shape: "Floorplan 3D", a view of the same layout.**
- Generated, never modelled: floors from polygons, walls extruded, doors and
  windows cut, stairs as stepped blocks, furniture as simple boxes by symbol.
  Devices stay bound to the layout.
- Same state language: light pools as coloured point lights or a floor
  decal, door open as a swung or red door, motion as a fading marker,
  night from `sun.sun`.
- Camera: fixed isometric-style orbit with a floor selector and a "cut-away"
  that hides upper floors. This also answers the floor3d bug about upper
  floors showing when a lower floor is selected.
- Delivery: a card option `view: 3d` and a toggle button on the card. Lazy
  loaded so the 2D card does not grow. three.js is about 600 KB minified; the
  repo rule is no runtime network, so it must be bundled, and it is a
  dependency decision for `docs/DECISIONS.md`.
- Honest limits: it will not look like a Blender render. No photoreal
  furniture, no shadows by default. Users who want that will keep using
  floor3d or renders. We win the people who "want the 3D look without a week
  of modelling" (one forum participant says exactly this).

**Risks.** (a) Phones and wall tablets: floor3d itself warns that shadows
and many lights hurt; our plan is to ship no dynamic shadows and cap lights,
but the performance is unmeasured. Prototype and measure on a real tablet
first. (b) A spec change: 3D is a stated non-goal, so this needs a decision
entry that supersedes it. (c) Scope: this is the size of a sprint, and the
risk is that it eats the 2D polish. (d) Demand is real but skewed to
showcase threads; I found no formal request with votes and no evidence users
want shadows or weather. Treat 3D as a growth feature, not a retention one.

**Cheaper alternative worth trying first: 2.5D.** The same renderer drawn as
oblique or isometric extrusion in SVG, no WebGL. Walls get a visible height,
rooms keep their paint, devices stay SVG icons. It keeps one draw path
(CLAUDE.md finding 8), costs a fraction of the bundle, works on any tablet,
and gives most of the "not a flat drawing" appeal. Houseplan already offers
a 2.5D view, so there is precedent. If the 2.5D prototype wins the reaction,
real 3D can follow.

## 4. Improvements, ranked by impact per cost

1. **Be found.** Fix the README images and status text, post to the HA
   community "Share your Projects", apply for the HACS default list.
   Afternoon. Nothing else matters until people can find it.
2. **Retake README and demo images; add a phone screenshot.** Same afternoon.
3. **Active list: default folded on narrow cards.** Half a task.
4. **Fit-to-room label shrink and leader lines for small rooms.** One task.
5. **Wall height in the schema** (`wallHeight` per floor, optional, default
   2.5 m) plus door and window heights. Needed for any 3D or 2.5D; also
   improves the assistant skill's output. Small, and safe without the view.
6. **2.5D view** as an experiment behind a flag. One sprint, measure the
   reaction.
7. **Real 3D** only if 2.5D lands well. One to two sprints plus performance
   work.
8. **First-run tour** in the editor, three steps.
9. **Floor summaries** (average temperature, lights on per floor) as an
   optional strip on the card.
10. **History replay** of motion and doors for the last day. Asked often on a
    rival; costs a recorder query. Only after the above.

## 5. New features that make sense

- **Scenes from the plan.** Tap a room, pick "all lights off", "movie". The
  plan already writes automations; this is the same plumbing.
- **Vacuum path and room cleaning from the plan.** Our vacuum type exists;
  the path and "clean this room" are what people post about.
- **Energy and climate layers.** A heat-map toggle from temperature sensors
  per room; a power-flow layer from plugs. Cheap, and it looks good.
- **Share a plan.** Export a read-only card config plus layout so other users
  can import the house of a friend. Fits the "discovery" problem.
- **Auto-place from areas.** A rival offers it. We have area links; placing
  new devices in their area's room by default would remove another step.
- **Import from Sweet Home 3D (`.sh3d`) or an IFC/DXF plan.** Many 3D users
  already have a model; reading its walls and rooms would pull them in
  without a modelling week.

## 6. What users do not seem to care about

Real-time shadows, furniture models and weather effects (only the 3D
projects themselves offer them). Many users are happy with plain 2D
picture-elements. The value stated in the threads is **ease of setup**, not
3D fidelity. That favours our approach.
